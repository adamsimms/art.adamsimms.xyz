description: |
  Opening a nondescript door in a Montreal high-rise, visitors step into a traditional Newfoundland saltbox hearth. The smell of wood and kettle steam meet a sense of warmth and hospitality, with an undertow of loss. The hearth is the saltbox’s heart and its central source of heat: a room that builds up and breaks apart through the day for cooking, crafts, and woodworking. Through a simulacrum of a window they see Pinchard’s Island itself, a live feed from Cloudberry, an autonomous camera system. Time and space collapse; the island feels suddenly near and still impossibly far. That tension is the work’s center.
body: |
  Pinchard’s Island, where Adam’s grandmother was born, was emptied when Canada’s post-1949 resettlement programs paid outport families to leave for “growth” communities. Cabin answers that distance by placing a fragment of homeland inside the city, then opening a live connection back to the island. Adam built the interior with his father from materials sourced in Newfoundland, furnishing it as a working hearth. He uses the room as a studio where people are welcomed for tea—so the installation stays a place people inhabit, not only a stage set to view.
