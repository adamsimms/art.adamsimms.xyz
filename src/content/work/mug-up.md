description: |
  At an outdoor table on Little Bay Islands, seemingly at the end of the earth, Adam and his grandmother Doris share a mug up: tea and a snack taken between meals, a ritual that belongs to the Newfoundland kitchen. Water laps the shore; gravel crunches underfoot as they set out Purity Crackers and tea cups. Once the mug up begins, a conversation about resettlement is erased from the soundscape; what remains is the inhaled affirmative yes of traditional Newfoundland dialect. No house frames them; home has already left the frame.
body: |
  At the time of filming, Little Bay Islands was undergoing resettlement to cut the cost of serving a remote outport. Mug Up, a video and sound performance, lifts the mug up from the home and asks what of Newfoundland culture and dialect survives uprooting, and what is already half-forgotten by those who still use it daily. On the ferry to and from the island, time won’t settle: the vessel feels still though it moves, until a swatch of sun slides along a bench and proves the minutes passing. Doris watches the horizon and barely shifts. Loss runs under the piece the way it runs under resettlement: time sped rather than savoured; a kitchen ritual set outside the house; a whole conversation pared to a single breath.
