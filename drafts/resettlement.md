description: |
  These photographs are from abandoned outport communities in Newfoundland emptied by government resettlement programs. Begun shortly after Newfoundland became Canada’s 10th province in 1949, the programs offered subsidies and improved public services to people relocated from isolated coastal communities to so-called “growth centres.” Names were struck from maps. As people dispersed, so did history, family relationships, livelihoods, and traditions.

  The images look familiar at first—landscapes, interiors—until the details turn: the iron bed frame against floral wallpaper, then the curtains in tatters. These were places judged to have “no great future.”

  What stays is absence. Homes meant to protect what people cherish; traditions meant to be sustained; a community meant to outlast any one life; the assumption you can always go home again. In these rooms that assumption doesn’t hold.
